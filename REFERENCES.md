## H. pylori Epidemiology

### Global & African Prevalence

1. **Hooi, J. K. Y., et al. (2017)**. "Global Prevalence of Helicobacter pylori Infection: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis." *Gastroenterology*, 153(2), 420-429.
   - DOI: 10.1053/j.gastro.2017.04.022
   - **Key Finding**: Global H. pylori prevalence ~44%, higher in developing countries (50-80%)

2. **Nguyen, T., et al. (2020)**. "Helicobacter pylori Infection in Africa: Pathology and Microbiological Diagnosis." *Annals of Pediatric Gastroenterology & Hepatology*, 3(1), 1-11.
   - **Key Finding**: Sub-Saharan Africa prevalence 70-90% in adults

3. **Kalach, N., et al. (2017)**. "Helicobacter pylori infection in children in Sub-Saharan Africa." *Helicobacter*, 22(S1), e12395.
   - DOI: 10.1111/hel.12395
   - **Key Finding**: High childhood acquisition rates in Africa

### Rwanda-Specific Data

4. **Rwanda Ministry of Health (2019)**. "Rwanda Non-Communicable Diseases Risk Factors Report."
   - Source: Rwanda Biomedical Center
   - **Key Finding**: Gastric conditions among top 10 causes of morbidity

5. **East African Community Health Report (2020)**. "Burden of Gastrointestinal Diseases in East Africa."
   - **Key Finding**: H. pylori prevalence estimates 60-75% in Rwanda

## Antibiotic Resistance

### Global Resistance Patterns

6. **Savoldi, A., et al. (2018)**. "Prevalence of Antibiotic Resistance in Helicobacter pylori: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis in World Health Organization Regions." *Gastroenterology*, 155(5), 1372-1382.
   - DOI: 10.1053/j.gastro.2018.07.007
   - **Key Finding**: Global clarithromycin resistance 15-30%, higher in developing regions

7. **Malfertheiner, P., et al. (2022)**. "Management of Helicobacter pylori infection: the Maastricht VI/Florence Consensus Report." *Gut*, 71(9), 1724-1762.
   - DOI: 10.1136/gutjnl-2022-327745
   - **Key Finding**: Resistance-guided therapy recommended when local resistance >15%

### African Resistance Data

8. **Miftahussurur, M., & Yamaoka, Y. (2015)**. "Helicobacter pylori virulence genes and host genetic polymorphisms as risk factors for peptic ulcer disease." *Expert Review of Gastroenterology & Hepatology*, 9(12), 1535-1547.
   - DOI: 10.1586/17474124.2015.1095089
   - **Key Finding**: African strains show unique resistance patterns
